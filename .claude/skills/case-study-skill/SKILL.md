# Customer Case Study Writer

Turn messy customer material into a case study that leads with the result, backs every claim with evidence, and reads like a person wrote it.

## Principles

1. **Impact first.** The headline and first paragraph state the outcome in numbers or concrete change. Context and backstory come after.
2. **Every claim has a source.** Each metric, quote and before/after statement traces to something the user gave you. Never invent numbers, quotes, job titles or timelines. If something is missing, ask or mark it `[NEEDS SOURCE]`.
3. **The customer is the hero.** The product is the tool they used. Write about what the customer did and achieved, not about features.
4. **Specific beats impressive.** "Cut month-end close from 9 days to 4" beats "dramatically improved efficiency".
5. **Plain language.** No buzzwords ("leverage", "seamless", "game-changer", "unlock"), no filler intros, no em-dash pileups.

## Workflow

### 1. Gather sources

Ask for, or read from the paths given, whatever exists:

| Source | What to pull from it |
| --- | --- |
| Customer interview / call transcript | Direct quotes, the problem in their words, decision moment, results they feel |
| CSM / account notes | Timeline, rollout, stakeholders, blockers, renewal/expansion |
| Slack / email threads | Unprompted praise, concrete wins, moments of friction that got solved |
| Usage / product data | Adoption, volume, time saved, before vs after metrics |
| Research (company site, press, LinkedIn, filings) | Company size, industry, what they do, public goals |

Also confirm: the audience (prospects in which segment?), the format (web page, PDF one-pager, slide, sales email snippet), the target length, and whether the customer has approved being named.

### 2. Build an evidence table

Before drafting, extract facts into a table and show it to the user:

| # | Claim | Type (metric / quote / fact) | Source | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Flag conflicts between sources (e.g., CSM notes say 40% faster, usage data shows 25%) and ask which is right. Prefer measured data over recollection.

### 3. Find the story

Pick the single strongest outcome as the spine. Identify:

- **Before:** the specific pain, with its cost (time, money, risk, missed growth)
- **Trigger:** why they acted now
- **Choice:** why this solution over alternatives (only if sources say so)
- **Rollout:** how it was adopted, briefly
- **After:** measured results, then qualitative change
- **Next:** expansion or what they plan to do next

### 4. Draft

Default structure (adjust to the format):

```
# [Customer] [achieved concrete result] with [product]

[1–2 sentence summary: who they are + headline result]

## At a glance
- Industry / size / region
- Key result 1 (number)
- Key result 2 (number)
- Key result 3 (number or concrete change)

## The challenge
[The pain in their words. Include the cost of the status quo.]

## The solution
[What they did, how they rolled it out. Keep product detail tied to the problem.]

## The results
[Metrics first, then one strong quote, then qualitative change.]

> "Quote that names a specific outcome." — Name, Title, Company

## What's next
[Optional: expansion, future plans.]
```

Short formats:
- **One-liner proof point:** `[Customer] [verb] [metric] in [timeframe] by [doing X].`
- **Sales email snippet:** 3 sentences: problem, result, link.
- **Slide:** headline result, 3 metric callouts, 1 quote, logo.

### 5. Review pass

Before handing over, check:

- [ ] Headline contains a concrete outcome
- [ ] Every number and quote matches the evidence table
- [ ] Quotes are verbatim or clearly marked as lightly edited for approval
- [ ] No `[NEEDS SOURCE]` left unflagged
- [ ] Reads naturally aloud; no marketing filler
- [ ] Customer name/logo use is approved (or anonymized as "a 500-person logistics company")

Deliver the draft plus a short list of open questions and the quotes/metrics that need customer sign-off.
